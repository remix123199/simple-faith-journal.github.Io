2 John 1:9–11 (GNT)

Staying Within the Teaching of Christ

Dr. Harris AndohBaptist International Worship Centre, Abelenkpe, Accra

Introduction

Across Africa today, the church is growing at a remarkable pace, and that is something to thank God for. Alongside this growth, however, there is a worrying trend. In many places, new teachings, prophetic “revelations,” spiritual formulas, and rituals are being added to what Christ taught. Some churches promote anointing oil, holy water, “miracle” handkerchiefs, and payment for blessings as means of receiving God’s favor. Others place visions, dreams, and the sayings of charismatic leaders on the same level as Scripture, or even above it. Still others blend Christianity with traditional customs and ancestral practices. This problem is not limited to Africa. It appears in many parts of the world, where human traditions, philosophies, and personal experiences are given authority equal to the Word of Christ.

The apostle John addressed this very danger in his second letter:

“Anyone who does not stay with the teaching of Christ, but goes beyond it, does not have God. Whoever does stay with the teaching has both the Father and the Son.”

2 John 1:9, GNT

The Dangers of Going Beyond the Scriptures

1. It separates a person from God

John is blunt: whoever goes beyond Christ’s teaching “does not have God.” This is not about minor differences of opinion. To add to Christ’s teaching is to move away from the very relationship that gives life. Fellowship with the Father and the Son is found only by remaining in what Christ taught.

2. It suggests that Christ’s teaching is incomplete

Anyone who adds to the words of Christ is, in effect, saying that what He gave us was not enough. Yet Jesus declared that He made known everything He received from the Father (John 15:15). Scripture warns that nothing should be added to God’s word (Deuteronomy 4:2; Revelation 22:18). To go beyond it is to place human wisdom above divine wisdom.

3. It opens the door to deception

When the Word of God is no longer the final standard, there is nothing to test teachers and teachings against. Experiences, emotions, and strong personalities become the measure of truth. Paul warned that some would turn away from the truth and follow teachers who say what people want to hear (2 Timothy 4:3–4). Without the anchor of Scripture, believers are easily “tossed about by every wind of teaching” (Ephesians 4:14).

4. It leads to exploitation of believers

Many false teachings come with a price tag. Where leaders claim special revelation that goes beyond Christ, ordinary believers can be pressured into paying for blessings, depending on a “man of God” for access to God, and living in fear. Christ, however, invites all to come to Him freely (Matthew 11:28).

5. It divides the church and weakens its witness

Teachings that go beyond Christ create factions, rivalries, and confusion. Instead of presenting one Lord, one faith, and one baptism (Ephesians 4:5), the church becomes a collection of competing voices, and the watching world is left wondering what the gospel really is.

6. It requires believers to be vigilant

John makes clear that this is not a matter to be treated casually. Verse 10 instructs believers not to welcome those who come without the true teaching of Christ, and not even to greet them with “Peace be with you.” Every believer has a responsibility to test what is taught against the Word, as the Bereans did when they examined the Scriptures daily to see whether Paul’s message was true (Acts 17:11).

Conclusion

John closes this passage with a sober warning: “For anyone who wishes them peace becomes their partner in the evil things they do” (2 John 1:11, GNT). Welcoming and endorsing teachers who go beyond the teaching of Christ is not a neutral act of kindness. It makes us participants in the damage their teaching does. Let every believer and every church therefore hold firmly to the words of Christ, test every teaching by the Scriptures, and refuse to give a platform to anything that adds to or departs from what He has taught, so that we may truly have both the Father and the Son.
